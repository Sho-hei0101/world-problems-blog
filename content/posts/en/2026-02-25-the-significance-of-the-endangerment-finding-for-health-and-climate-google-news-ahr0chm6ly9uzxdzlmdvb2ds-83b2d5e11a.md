## Problem overview

The endangerment finding, established in 2009, determined that greenhouse gas emissions pose a threat to public health and welfare. This finding was crucial as it provided the legal foundation for the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) to regulate emissions. Since then, the implications for both health and the climate have been profound, affecting legislation, public awareness, and global climate agreements. However, ongoing political debates and challenges to environmental regulations continue to shape the landscape of climate action and public health.

## Why this matters globally

Globally, climate change is recognized as one of the most significant threats to public health. The endangerment finding is essential because it links climate action to health outcomes, emphasizing that reducing emissions can lead to cleaner air, fewer respiratory diseases, and overall improved health. As climate change exacerbates issues like heatwaves, flooding, and vector-borne diseases, understanding the endangerment finding becomes crucial for developing effective policies that protect vulnerable populations worldwide.

## FAQ

**What is the endangerment finding?**

The endangerment finding is a determination by the EPA that greenhouse gases pose a threat to public health and welfare.

**Why is the endangerment finding important for public health?**

It establishes a legal basis for regulating emissions, which can lead to improved air quality and health outcomes.

**How does climate change affect health?**

Climate change can lead to respiratory diseases, heat-related illnesses, and the spread of infectious diseases.

**What can individuals do to help combat climate change?**

Individuals can reduce their carbon footprint, advocate for policy changes, and support sustainable practices.

**Is the endangerment finding still in effect?**

Yes, but it faces ongoing political challenges and debates regarding its enforcement and implications.
