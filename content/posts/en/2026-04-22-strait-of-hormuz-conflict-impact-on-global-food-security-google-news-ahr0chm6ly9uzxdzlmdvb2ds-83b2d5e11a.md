## Problem overview

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical maritime chokepoint for global energy supplies, with approximately 20% of the world's oil passing through it. Recent conflicts in the region have raised concerns about the stability of energy supplies, which in turn affects agricultural production globally. Fertilizer, a key input for agriculture, relies heavily on energy prices. As tensions rise, both fertilizer and energy prices have surged, leading to fears of food insecurity worldwide. This situation poses a significant risk to food systems, particularly in regions that are heavily dependent on imported food and agricultural inputs.

## Why this matters globally

The implications of the Strait of Hormuz conflict extend far beyond the immediate region. As energy prices rise due to geopolitical tensions, the cost of inputs for agricultural production also increases. This can lead to higher food prices, reduced access to essential food items, and increased hunger rates, particularly in developing countries. The interconnectedness of global food systems means that disruptions in one region can have cascading effects worldwide, highlighting the need for proactive measures to ensure food security.
