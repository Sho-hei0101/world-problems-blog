## Problem overview

As global temperatures continue to rise, scientists are increasingly concerned about the frequency and intensity of summer heat spikes. These extreme weather events not only pose risks to human health but also threaten ecosystems, agriculture, and water resources. Recent studies indicate that we are edging closer to a critical threshold of 2°C above pre-industrial levels, which could lead to catastrophic consequences if not addressed promptly.

## Why this matters globally

The implications of rising temperatures are not confined to one region; they are a global issue affecting millions. Heat spikes can exacerbate droughts, increase the risk of wildfires, and lead to heat-related illnesses. Furthermore, vulnerable populations, especially in developing countries, may lack the resources to adapt to these changes. ## FAQ

**What are heat spikes?**

Heat spikes are short periods of excessively high temperatures that can lead to health risks and environmental stress.

**How does global warming lead to heat spikes?**

Global warming increases average temperatures, which can intensify weather patterns and lead to more frequent and severe heat events.

**What can individuals do to help reduce global warming?**

Individuals can reduce their carbon footprint by using public transport, conserving energy, and supporting renewable energy initiatives.

**Why is a 2°C increase significant?**

A 2°C increase is considered a critical threshold that could lead to severe environmental impacts, including extreme weather events and loss of biodiversity.

**Are there policies in place to combat climate change?**

Yes, many countries have implemented policies aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions and promoting sustainability, but more action is needed.
