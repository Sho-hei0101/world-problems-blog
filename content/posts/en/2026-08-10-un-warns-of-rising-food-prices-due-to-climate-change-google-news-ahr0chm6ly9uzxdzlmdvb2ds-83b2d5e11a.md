## Problem overview

The recent warnings from the UN signal a concerning trend in the global food market, where rising food prices are increasingly attributed to the dual pressures of climate change and escalating energy costs. As extreme weather events become more frequent and energy prices fluctuate, the cost of producing and transporting food is expected to rise significantly, impacting food security worldwide. This situation poses a grave threat, particularly to vulnerable populations who are already struggling to access adequate nutrition.

## Why this matters globally

The implications of rising food prices due to climate change and energy price hikes extend far beyond individual households. Globally, food security is at risk, leading to potential social unrest, increased poverty levels, and a rise in malnutrition. Countries that rely heavily on food imports will face severe economic challenges, and the interconnectedness of global supply chains means that the effects will be felt across borders. Addressing this issue is critical for maintaining stability and ensuring that all individuals have access to sufficient food resources.

## Practical actions you can take

Addressing the challenges posed by rising food prices requires a multifaceted approach. Here are some practical actions individuals, communities, and policymakers can take to mitigate the impact of these rising costs:

- Stay informed about local food prices and trends.
- Support local farmers' markets and buy in-season produce.
- Reduce food waste by planning meals and using leftovers.
- Advocate for policies that support sustainable agriculture.
- Participate in community discussions about food security.
- Educate yourself and others about the impact of climate change on food systems.
- Explore alternative food sources like community gardens.
