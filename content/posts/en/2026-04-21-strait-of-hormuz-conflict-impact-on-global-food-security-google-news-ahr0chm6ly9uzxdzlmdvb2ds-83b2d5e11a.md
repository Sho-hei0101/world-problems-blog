## Problem overview

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical maritime chokepoint through which a significant portion of the world's oil and gas supplies transit. Recent conflicts in the region have raised tensions, leading to increased prices for both energy and fertilizers. These price surges have profound implications for global food security, particularly in nations that rely heavily on imports for their food supply. The interdependence of energy costs and agricultural productivity creates a precarious situation for many economies worldwide.

## Why this matters globally

The global economy is intricately linked, and disruptions in a key region can have cascading effects across multiple sectors. As energy prices rise, the cost of fertilizers—essential for food production—also climbs. This can lead to decreased agricultural output, increased food prices, and ultimately, food insecurity. Understanding these dynamics is vital for policymakers and stakeholders aiming to mitigate the impacts of geopolitical conflicts on food systems.
