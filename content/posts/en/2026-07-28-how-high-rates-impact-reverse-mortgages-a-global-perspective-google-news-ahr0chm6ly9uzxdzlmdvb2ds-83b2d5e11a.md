## Problem overview

The rise in interest rates has created a unique challenge for the reverse mortgage industry. Traditionally seen as a financial tool for seniors, reverse mortgages allow homeowners to convert part of their home equity into cash. However, with increasing rates, the dynamics of this financial product are shifting. High rates can lead to decreased demand, affecting both lenders and borrowers. Homeowners may find themselves facing higher costs, while lenders adjust their offerings to cope with the changing economic landscape. ## FAQ

**What is a reverse mortgage?**

A reverse mortgage is a loan that allows homeowners, typically seniors, to convert part of their home equity into cash without having to sell their home.

**How do rising interest rates affect reverse mortgages?**

Higher interest rates can increase the costs associated with reverse mortgages, leading to decreased demand and potentially less favorable terms for borrowers.

**Are reverse mortgages safe?**

While reverse mortgages can be a useful financial tool, they come with risks. It's important to consult with a financial advisor to understand the implications fully.

**Can I lose my home with a reverse mortgage?**

Yes, if you fail to meet the obligations of the loan, such as paying property taxes, homeowners insurance, or maintaining the home, you risk foreclosure.

**What are the alternatives to reverse mortgages?**

Alternatives include traditional home equity loans, selling the home, or exploring government assistance programs for seniors.
