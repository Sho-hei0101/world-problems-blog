## Problem overview

As climate change accelerates, many regions are experiencing extreme weather patterns characterized by alternating periods of severe drought and intense flooding. This phenomenon, often referred to as 'drought-flood whiplash,' poses significant risks to public health, agriculture, and water supply. Communities that once experienced predictable weather patterns are now facing unpredictability, leading to challenges in resource management and emergency preparedness.

## Why this matters globally

The intensification of drought-flood cycles is not just a local or national concern, but a global one. Countries around the world are witnessing the adverse effects of climate change, which include increased health risks such as waterborne diseases, heat-related illnesses, and mental health challenges stemming from displacement and disaster. Understanding these impacts is essential for developing effective public health strategies and fostering resilience in vulnerable communities.

## FAQ

**What is drought-flood whiplash?**

Drought-flood whiplash refers to the rapid alternation between periods of drought and flooding due to climate change.

**How does climate change contribute to these weather patterns?**

Climate change alters precipitation patterns, leading to more extreme weather events, including droughts and floods.

**What are the health risks associated with drought and flooding?**

Health risks include waterborne diseases, heat stress, and mental health issues due to displacement and disaster recovery.

**How can communities prepare for these extreme weather events?**
