## Problem overview

As the Iran war escalates, the IMF has identified a significant rise in energy prices, labeling it as a 'sudden tax' on the global economy. This surge in prices is not merely a localized issue but has far-reaching implications for countries worldwide, affecting everything from consumer spending to inflation rates. The conflict has disrupted supply chains and created uncertainty in oil markets, leading to increased costs for businesses and households alike. This situation raises concerns about economic stability and growth, especially for nations that rely heavily on energy imports.

## Why this matters globally

The impact of rising energy prices extends beyond immediate financial concerns. Globally, higher prices can lead to inflation, reducing consumers' purchasing power and creating a ripple effect across various sectors. Economies that are already fragile may face even greater challenges, potentially leading to recession in the worst-case scenarios. Furthermore, developing nations, which often lack the financial resilience to absorb such shocks, may experience heightened social unrest and economic hardship. Understanding the global interconnectedness of energy prices is crucial for policymakers and citizens alike.
